# "Hey Siri, log an expense in Murmur" (Sep 20, 2026)

The owner's idea, in his words: standing outside Walmart, say it and never
take the phone out. This is what shipped, what iOS allows, and where the
seams are.

## The flow

```
"Hey Siri, log an expense in Murmur"
Siri: "What did you spend?"
"five dollars at Walmart"
Siri: "Saved. $5.00 at Walmart."
```

Two turns, both hands free, screen locked, app never opened. Nothing to
set up: the phrase works the moment the app is installed.

## Why two turns and not one

An App Shortcut phrase can embed a parameter only when that parameter is
an `AppEnum` or an `AppEntity`. Free text cannot go in the phrase, so
"log five dollars at Walmart in Murmur" as one utterance is not something
iOS offers a third-party app. Apple also requires the app name inside
every phrase, so the bare "log five dollars at Walmart" will never reach
us.

The single-utterance version exists only through SiriKit's Lists and
Notes domain ("add milk to Things"), which Apple **deprecated at WWDC
2026** in favour of App Intents, with compile-time warnings and a stated
two to three year window. Building Murmur's newest surface on a retiring
API was not a trade worth making, so the feature is App Intents only.

What the user can still do, if they want one word: in Shortcuts, wrap
`Log an expense` in a shortcut named anything ("Spent"), or put it on the
Action button.

## Architecture: one queue, two producers

Siri reuses the Apple Pay capture pipeline that has been live since
Aug 17, 2026, rather than opening a second path to the database.

```
SiriLogExpense.swift  ──┐
                        ├─► Documents/wallet-capture-queue.jsonl ─► WalletCaptureDrain
WalletCapture.swift   ──┘        (one JSON object per line)          (parse, save, undo)
   (Apple Pay)
```

- The intent writes `{ kind: "phrase", phrase: "five dollars at Walmart" }`
  and wakes JavaScript through the existing `NotificationCenter` pair, now
  carrying a `dialog` string back so Siri speaks the row the app actually
  wrote instead of a canned line.
- The drain parses the sentence with `parseExpense`, the same parser the
  microphone uses, and saves through `createTransaction` with
  `source: 'voice'` and the sentence as `raw_transcript`. Siri and the mic
  cannot disagree, and income is detected the same way ("I got paid 200"
  lands as a credit).
- Budget: the intent waits 9 s, the parse gets 6 s of that. Past it Siri
  says "Got it. Murmur will file it." and the drain files it on the next
  launch or foreground, posting a notification so a late save is not
  silent.
- No amount in the sentence and no parse? Nothing is saved and Siri says
  so. A phantom row is worse than a repeated sentence.
- A recurring rule is never created from Siri: one unheard sentence must
  not write months of future rows.

## The cold start, fixed while building this

A capture arrives precisely when the app is *not* running, and both the
profile and the category list live in a process-lifetime memory cache: on
a background launch both are empty and both need the network. That had two
consequences, neither visible in the UI:

- **Currency.** The drain fell back to USD, English and UTC, silently
  writing the wrong `currency_code` for anyone outside the dollar zone, on
  exactly the entries the feature exists for.
- **Category.** With no categories to resolve the parser's suggestion
  against, every background entry saved uncategorised. The row was right
  and the chip was missing.

The drain now writes currency, locale, timezone and the category list to
disk whenever it has them, and reads them back when it does not
(`services/capturePrefs.ts`). Sign-out clears the file, so the next
account never inherits the previous one's currency or categories. Apple
Pay capture gets the same fix, since it had the same bug.

## Localisation

Siri matches phrases literally, so a French phone with French Siri never
matches an English phrase. Each language gets six phrases in
`<lang>.lproj/AppShortcuts.strings`, plus the intent's own strings in
`Localizable.strings`, and `CFBundleLocalizations` declares en, fr, es,
pt.

One trap worth recording: adding those files individually to the Xcode
project silently drops most of them. Expo's `addResourceFileToGroup`
matches group children by basename, so the second `AppShortcuts.strings`
looks like a duplicate and is skipped, which would have shipped English
phrases to every language while looking correct in the diff. The plugin
adds each `<lang>.lproj` as a **folder reference** instead, which Xcode
copies into the bundle verbatim.

## Shipped

## On the device, once the build lands

1. Settings > Ask Siri shows the phrase.
2. Lock the phone, say it, answer, listen for the amount and merchant.
3. Open Murmur: the row is on Today with the sentence as its transcript.
4. Airplane mode: say "twelve dollars at Shell". It saves with the amount
   and no merchant, and syncs later.

## Device test, Sep 20 2026 (build 64), and what it changed

The owner used it at home and three things came back.

1. **The date was wrong.** Both Siri entries landed on the previous
   evening at 7pm. The parser answers a sentence with no time as midnight
   UTC, which is yesterday evening anywhere west of London; the confirm
   sheet has repaired that since build 8 and this path skipped the repair.
   `normaliseSpoken` now runs the parsed timestamp through
   `normalizeParsedTransactedAt` and falls back to the moment Siri heard
   it. Three regression tests.
2. **Merchant casing.** "target" from the microphone sat above "Target"
   from Siri: the same shop twice. Casing is now normalised at the parse
   boundary (`validateParsedExpense`), so every source agrees. Names that
   already mix case (iPhone, eBay, McDonald's) and short all-caps
   acronyms (KFC, BP, IKEA) are left alone.
3. **"You are forcing one exact sentence."** Fair. There are now twelve
   phrases per language instead of six, including short ones ("Murmur
   expense") and app-name-first ones. A test keeps the Swift list and the
   translation keys in lockstep, because a drift there silently ships
   English phrases to every other language.

Also from that session: the first time you say the phrase, iOS shows a
**Turn on** card, and answering into that interruption sends the next
sentence to plain Siri (the owner's went to Maps). That is one-time and
not something an app can suppress, so Settings > Ask Siri now says it in
one line.

## Second device test, build 65

The date and the casing were confirmed fixed on the owner's phone. Two
things came out of it.

**Income had no door.** "Hey Siri, log a source of income in Murmur"
answered "I can't help you with that", because all twelve phrases said
*expense*. Murmur has always been one capture flow with the model deciding
income or expense, and Siri was the one place that forgot it. There is now
a second App Shortcut, `LogSpokenIncomeIntent`, with eight phrases per
language and its own question ("What came in?"). Both doors queue the same
sentence and go through the same parser and the same save; the door is
carried as a `hint` and leads the transcript in the user's language
("Revenu reçu : deux cents de la part d'Acme"), because "two hundred from
Acme" is income or a payment out depending only on which phrase was used.
What gets stored as the transcript stays verbatim.

**Every Siri save now posts a notification.** It used to post one only
when the save finished after Siri had given up, on the reasoning that the
spoken line was enough. The owner's point is better: the voice is gone the
moment Siri stops talking, it cannot carry Undo or Edit, and something
happened in the app while the app was closed, so the app should say so.
Apple Pay capture already worked this way. It stays silent while Murmur is
on screen, where the undo toast is the confirmation.

## On one utterance, decided Sep 21 2026

"Hey Siri, log five dollars at Walmart in Murmur" as a single sentence is
not available to any third-party app. App Shortcut phrases accept only
`AppEnum` and `AppEntity` parameters, never open-ended text, in iOS 26 as
in iOS 16, and Apple requires the app name in the phrase. The only route
is SiriKit's Lists and Notes domain, which Apple deprecated in June 2026.

Not taken, deliberately. It would mean a second target, an App Group that
the working Apple Pay queue would have to move into, another provisioning
profile, and teaching users a sentence Apple removes in two to three
years. That is a large change to a live app to save one spoken turn, on
an API with an expiry date. If Apple ships a finance or note-taking App
Schema, one utterance becomes possible properly and this is the first
thing to revisit.

## Open

- **Apple Intelligence schemas.** There is no finance or note-taking
  schema today, so nothing to adopt. Worth re-checking each WWDC: a
  matching schema is the only route to one-utterance natural language.
- **Android.** No equivalent; App Actions are a different build.
- **The phrase list** is six per language. If a phrase feels unnatural on
  the device, it is one line in `native/ios/siri-phrases.js` and a rebuild.

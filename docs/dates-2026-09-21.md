# The date a transaction carries (Sep 21, 2026)

Owner, after using Siri in the field: money is recorded on the day you
think of it, not the day it moved. A salary that lands on the 7th gets
entered on the 9th. A phone bill due on the 14th gets set up today. Until
now the app could hear a date but never let anyone fix one.

## What was true before

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Spoken dates | Parsed. "on the 14th", "last Friday", "yesterday" all reach `transacted_at`, and a date with no time is repaired to midday local instead of midnight UTC. |
| Confirm sheet | Showed the date, **read only**. The code said so: "a date picker is deliberately out of scope for this pass." |
| Edit screen | No date field at all. Amount, merchant, note, category, payment method, recurring, and nothing about when. |
| Recurring rules | Anchored to `transacted_at` (migration 013). So a wrong date did not mislabel one row, it scheduled every future one on the wrong day. |

## What changed

- **`DateTimeField`** (`src/components/DateTimeField.tsx`): Apple's own
  compact controls, one for the date and one for the time, side by side.
  Tapping either opens the system calendar or clock. Nothing about dates
  is reinvented, which is the point: people already know how this works.
  Android opens the platform dialogs in sequence, date then time.
- **The confirm sheet** uses it. A date heard from the sentence is the
  starting value and can be corrected before saving. An untouched sheet
  still writes null, so `createTransaction` stamps the real save moment
  rather than the instant the sheet happened to open.
- **The edit screen** uses it, above the payment row, and passes
  `transacted_at` through `editTransaction`.
- **`transacted_at` is now an updatable column.** `local_day` and
  `occurrence_date` are derived from it on read and on push, so both
  follow without a migration.
- **The FX snapshot is re-dated** when the date of a foreign-currency row
  moves: the rate belongs to the day the money moved. A row in the
  profile's own currency never needs it, and an offline edit keeps the old
  snapshot rather than guessing.
- **A recurring schedule moves with its template.** Correcting a phone
  bill from the 21st to the 14th passes `starts_at` to `updateRule`, which
  re-anchors the day, weekday and time and clears `last_generated`, so the
  new date is the next occurrence rather than one cadence past it.

## What this does not do yet

**Two fixed days a month** ("paid on the 7th and the 21st") cannot be
expressed. The frequency enum is daily, weekly, biweekly, monthly,
quarterly, yearly, and biweekly is every 14 days, which drifts away from
fixed days of the month. Semi-monthly is a real schedule that a lot of
people are paid on, and supporting it means a new frequency value in the
CHECK constraint, in the generator (Edge function and mobile catch-up), in
the rule editor and in four languages. Worth doing; it is its own piece of
work, not a line in this one.

Until then, the honest workaround inside the product is two monthly rules,
one anchored to the 7th and one to the 21st.
